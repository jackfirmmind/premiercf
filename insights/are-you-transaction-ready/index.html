Guide for owners

Are you ready for a sale?

Chances to sell or raise investment arrive when they are ready, not when you are. It pays to build readiness into how you run the business every day.

By Rupert Lewis, Partner

In this guide

  1. What readiness means
  2. Have a robust business plan, and renew it each year
  3. Empower your management team to lead
  4. Address the big issues today
  5. Next steps

What readiness means

Transaction readiness

A state where you can present your business to a buyer or investor, complete the deal in the shortest time, and get the highest value.

At some point, most shareholders will either look for investment or sell their shares. Investment can come from banks, venture capital or private equity. A sale can be to a trade buyer, to the management team, or to a private equity investor.

This may come from your own strategic plan, or from a buyer who approaches you directly. Either way, being ready means you get more value from your business and the deal takes less time.

Getting ready should start now, and become part of how you run the business. There are 10 areas to focus on. This guide explains 3 of them.

1 Have a robust business plan, and renew it each year

A plan keeps the business focused. It also shows an investor where the business has come from and where it is going.

Write the plan in language everyone can understand. It should mix the realistic with the ambitious.

2 Empower your management team to lead

This matters for 2 reasons.

  1. You will be busy during a transaction, so others must run the business day to day.
  2. If you are leaving, or if the business depends on you alone, buyers need to see the people who will run it after you go.

3 Address the big issues today

Big issues take time to fix. If you leave them, they can lower the value of your business, or cause the deal to fail.

A failed deal is costly and painful. You can avoid it by starting early.
