The shareholders' strategic plan

A clear plan, agreed by the shareholders and renewed each year, is the best way to maximise their return.

By Premier Corporate Finance

What the plan is

Shareholders' strategic plan

A document that sets out the result the shareholders want, the timescale, and who is responsible for delivering it.

Shareholders often disagree. That is normal. But you can only succeed when you plan to succeed.

Shareholder value matters most at exit, whether that is a share sale or a business sale. The return is highest when a plan has guided the business towards it.

4 steps to a plan that works

Whether you plan to exit in 6 months or in several years, these 4 steps come from our experience with shareholders.

1 Write the plan, then debate and agree it

Putting the plan on paper starts a debate. That is good. Through the debate, shareholders agree the goals. The plan needs enough detail to show the goals, timescales, milestones and resources.

2 Give people responsibility for it

Shareholders must make named leaders responsible for delivering the plan. Those leaders set targets and timetables. Shareholders check progress at regular reviews.

3 Get an outside review

A plan written only from inside the business can give a skewed view. An outside adviser can spot gaps and grey areas, and connect you with people who can help deliver it.

4 Monitor progress

Once the plan is in place, track progress against the milestones. The route may change, and you may need guidance along the way.
