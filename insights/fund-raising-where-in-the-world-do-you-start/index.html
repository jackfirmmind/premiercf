Raising finance: where do you start?

Money is rarely the problem. Knowing where to look, and when, is. This guide explains how to find the likely investors among all the possible ones.

By Rupert Lewis, Partner

In this guide

  1. Many options, many filters
  2. The scattergun approach
  3. Use networks and incubators
  4. Plan your fundraising
  5. Protect your cash flow
  6. Take a partner

Many options, many filters

There are many sources of funding: grants, crowdfunding, angel investors, venture capital, private equity, banks, sector funds and incubators.

Each one also has its own rules on who it funds. They look at size, age, revenue, profit, sector and region. The real task is to find the probable investors among the possible ones.

The scattergun approach

Some businesses send their pitch to as many investors as possible and hope one says yes. This can work, and it gets your name known.

But it takes a lot of time and energy. It pulls founders away from customers, staff and sales. If you take this route, do it with a co-founder who shares the workload.

Use networks and incubators

Business hubs and incubators connect you with advisers, funders and other businesses in one place. This shortens the networking a great deal.

Established businesses already have a network of financial and legal advisers. These people are often connected to banks and investors, so use them. Your bank can be your first call, but not your only one.

Plan your fundraising

A better approach is to plan the funding round before you start. Research which funders are most likely to invest, what they have backed before, and what rules they apply. Then focus your time on them.

This also lets you move on quickly if one says no. When an investor says no, they mean no, unless you have something new to show them.

Look for an investor partner, not just an investor. The right partner knows your sector, has useful contacts, and will stand by you when times are hard. That can be worth more than the money.

Protect your cash flow

Cash pressure can push you into rushed decisions. Under pressure, you risk giving away more of the business than you should. It can be better to cut costs and buy time to find the right funding.

Take a partner

Plan ahead and do not do it alone. A good adviser helps you understand the funding options, test your financial model, prepare your pitch, and supports you to the end.
