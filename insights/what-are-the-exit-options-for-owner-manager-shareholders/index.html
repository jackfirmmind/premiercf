Guide for owners

What are your exit options?

Every owner-manager will one day want to release the value in their shares. The first challenge is choosing the right route out.

By Premier Corporate Finance

In this guide

  1. Start with what you want
  2. Understand why each buyer is buying
  3. Show buyers what they value
  4. Keep running the business
  5. Choose the right adviser

Start with what you want

Many owners receive letters from companies offering to buy their business or release their equity. Do not rush into one route. Compare every option against your own goals first.

There are 4 main exit routes for the shareholders of an owner-managed business:

  • a sale to a trade buyer;
  • investment from private equity or venture capital;
  • a management buy-out or buy-in;
  • refinancing through a bank or other lender.

Each route leads to a very different outcome. So ask yourself one question first: what do I want from the sale of my shares?

The answers vary. You may want to walk away, retire, stay involved without shares, stay involved with shares, start a new venture, or bring in a backer to grow faster. Or simply ski more.

Understand why each buyer is buying

Every buyer has different reasons to buy. To get the best value, present your business so that it:

  1. attracts strong interest from suitable buyers;
  2. shows each buyer the value your business brings to them;
  3. attracts credible buyers who have the money to complete.

To do this, research the buyers and learn what they value. It may be your services, products, technology, sector knowledge, growth, locations, intellectual property or leadership team.

Buyers do not always see this value for themselves. Help them see it. When buyers see the worth of your business, they are more likely to pay the price it deserves.

Show buyers what they value

These factors matter to almost every buyer. Cover them in your sale documents:

  • The potential of the business in its market.
  • A clear, credible story of where the business has come from and where it can go.
  • A strong team that can lead the next stage of growth. The buyer is betting on these people.
  • Reliable financial information, with a summary and the detail behind it. This builds confidence and speeds up due diligence.
  • Plans for the risks a buyer may raise. This protects the value, and the deal.
  • Room to grow beyond the current business, for example into new sectors, locations or technologies.
  • A good understanding of your clients, competitors and market.

Keep running the business

Selling a business is a heavy load for the shareholders. One of the biggest value killers is a management team that focuses on the sale instead of the business.

When that happens, clients and staff notice, and sales fall. No buyer wants to see that. Make sure the business keeps the people and attention it needs throughout the sale.

Choose the right adviser

Plan ahead and do not do it alone. A good adviser feels like part of your team from the first meeting. Ask 3 questions:

  • Sector experience. Do they know the buyers in your sector?
  • Deal experience. Has the person in front of you done this before, and will they see it through?
  • Fit. Will they be there when you need them?
