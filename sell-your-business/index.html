How the sale process works

Most sales take 3 to 6 months from our first meeting to completion. A partner leads every stage.

  1. Valuation and readiness

    We meet you, review your accounts and the business, and give you a realistic value range. We tell you what would increase that value before you go to market.

    You receiveA value range and a list of actions to raise it.

  2. Preparation

    We write the information memorandum. This is the document that presents your business to buyers. We also prepare the financial information and set up a secure data room.

    You receiveAn information memorandum, an anonymous profile and a data room.

  3. Buyer approach

    We build a list of trade and private equity buyers in the UK and abroad. Each buyer sees an anonymous profile first. They sign a confidentiality agreement before they learn your name.

    You receiveA shortlist of interested buyers under confidentiality agreements.

  4. Offers and negotiation

    We run the process so buyers compete. We compare price, deal structure, deferred payments and plans for your staff. Then we negotiate heads of terms with the buyer you prefer.

    You receiveSigned heads of terms with the buyer you choose.

  5. Due diligence and completion

    The buyer checks the business in detail. We manage their questions, work with your lawyers and accountants, and keep the timetable on track.

    You receiveA completed sale.

We score every buyer before you meet them

In the last 3 years there have been more than 280 deals in the UK TIC sector, by more than 30 buyers. Not all of them are right for you. We score each one on 12 factors, so you only meet the committed buyers.

What we check

  • Whether the buyer has the cash in place, or still needs to raise it.
  • How fast they make decisions, and how heavy their due diligence is.
  • Whether they have a history of cutting the price late in the deal.
  • Whether they keep your business and name, or absorb it fully.

A buyer who needs your business pays more than a buyer who only wants it. Scoring shows us which is which.

What buyers pay for in a testing and inspection business

Buyers pay more for a business that is easy to own. These are the six factors we check first.

  • Accreditations

    UKAS and other accreditations that stay with the business after the sale. Buyers check they are current and not tied to one person.

  • Repeat revenue

    Contracted or recurring work. Framework agreements and long customer relationships increase the value.

  • Customer spread

    No single customer should be a large share of revenue. High dependence on one customer lowers the price.

  • Qualified people

    Certified technicians and engineers who will stay after the sale. Buyers are often buying the team.

  • A management team

    A business that runs day to day without the owner. This makes the handover easier and the price higher.

  • Clear financial records

    Clean accounts, monthly management figures and a sensible forecast. This makes due diligence faster.

Questions owners ask

If your question is not here, call a partner on 07768 936886.

What does it cost?

We work on a success-only fee. You pay our fee when the deal completes. The first conversation and the valuation carry no cost and no obligation.

Will my staff and customers find out?

Not until you decide. Buyers first see an anonymous profile. They sign a confidentiality agreement before they learn your name. We agree with you when and how to tell your staff.

How long does a sale take?

Most sales take 3 to 6 months from our first meeting to completion. Good preparation is the part that shortens it most.

Do I have to stay after the sale?

It depends on the buyer and the deal structure. Many buyers ask the owner to stay for a handover period. Part of the price may be paid later, based on results. We negotiate these terms for you.

Can I sell only part of the business?

Yes. You can sell a stake to an investor, or release some of your equity and keep control. See raising finance and equity release.

What do you need from me to start?

Your last three years of accounts and your latest management figures. That is enough for a first valuation.
